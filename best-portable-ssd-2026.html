What's changed this year

Portable SSD prices have been unusually volatile in 2026 because of a NAND flash supply squeeze driven largely by AI infrastructure demand — see our full price watch for the numbers. Practically, that means the "best" drive is now as much about what's actually in stock at a sane price as it is about raw specs. The picks below are chosen for being consistently available, not just fastest on paper.

Our picks

Best overall

1–2TB mainstream portable SSD

USB 3.2 Gen 2 (10Gbps) · read speeds around 1,000MB/s · pocket-sized, no external power needed

The sweet spot for most people: fast enough to edit photos or move large files directly off it, small enough to keep in a bag permanently. At this tier, prioritize a drive with a real metal or reinforced case — portable SSDs get dropped, and the flash inside is worth protecting.

Example: Samsung T7 Shield 2TB (USB 3.2 Gen 2, IP65-rated rugged case)

Best budget

500GB–1TB entry SSD

USB 3.2 Gen 1 (5Gbps) · read speeds around 400–540MB/s

Fine for backups, game libraries, or moving files between machines where top speed doesn't matter. With NAND prices up sharply this year, entry-tier capacity has gotten proportionally more expensive than it was in 2024–2025 — factor that into whether "budget" still saves you as much as it used to.

How to compare specs that actually matter

SpecWhy it matters
Interface (USB 3.2 Gen 1/2/2x2, Thunderbolt)Sets your real-world speed ceiling — a fast drive on a slow port performs like a slow drive
Sustained vs. burst speedMarketing numbers are usually burst speed; sustained speed matters more for large transfers
Case materialMetal/rugged cases survive drops; plastic ones are lighter but more fragile
Warranty length3+ years is standard for reputable brands; shorter warranties are a signal worth noticing

Only need something small?

For quick transfers rather than backups, a USB flash drive is cheaper and just as fast for everyday use.
